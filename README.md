1. Introduction and Topic

This project examines judicial decisions concerning maintenance under Section 125 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, delivered between 1 September 2021 and 31 August 2026. Section 125 is a social welfare provision intended to prevent destitution by providing maintenance to eligible wives, children and parents who are unable to maintain themselves.

Our team selected this topic because maintenance disputes involve important questions of financial responsibility, gender justice, family relationships and access to justice. We aim to understand how courts have interpreted and applied this provision and identify patterns in judicial decision-making over the selected period.

2. How We Found the Judgments

The judgments were identified using Indian Kanoon and available court-related judgment resources. The primary search terms included “Section 125 CrPC”, “maintenance under Section 125”, “wife maintenance”, “maintenance of parents”, “divorced wife maintenance” and “Section 125 CrPC Supreme Court judgments”.

The research dataset contains 20 judgments decided between September 2021 and August 2026. Cases were selected based on their relevance to Section 125 CrPC and the issues arising from its interpretation and application. The dataset records the case name, citation or case number, date of decision, bench strength, nature of proceedings, outcome, main legal provision, issue, holding and precedent relied upon.

The research covers judgments attributed to the Supreme Court of India and various High Courts. Only decisions relevant to the selected legal provision were considered for inclusion.

3. What the Data Shows

Chart 1: Judgments Per Year

The yearly distribution shows that 2021 recorded 3 judgments, 2022 recorded 5, 2023 recorded 2, 2024 recorded 5, 2025 recorded 2 and 2026 recorded 3 judgments. The highest number of cases in the dataset occurred in 2022 and 2024. The variation indicates that the number of relevant decisions recorded was not consistent throughout the research period. One interesting observation is that 2024 witnessed the same number of judgments as 2022 despite being a different stage of the research period.

Chart 2: Outcome of Judgments

The outcome distribution records 4 judgments as allowed, 1 as dismissed, 3 as partly allowed and 12 as other. The relatively high number of cases classified as ‘Other’ is noteworthy. It indicates that the recorded outcomes do not always fall neatly into the categories of allowed, dismissed or partly allowed. This also highlights the importance of examining the actual relief granted rather than relying exclusively on procedural outcomes.

Chart 3: Type of Cases

The classification by case type shows 13 criminal appeals, 5 civil appeals and 2 Special Leave Petitions (SLPs). Criminal appeals form the largest category, representing the majority of the dataset. This suggests that disputes concerning maintenance under Section 125 frequently reach the courts through criminal appellate proceedings. The absence of writ petitions in the recorded dataset is another notable observation.

4. Limitations of the Study

This dataset consists of only 20 judgments and should not be treated as a complete representation of all litigation concerning Section 125 CrPC across India. The findings are descriptive and cannot establish nationwide judicial trends or predict future outcomes. Further, differences in court jurisdiction, factual circumstances and procedural stages may influence the decisions and limit direct comparisons.
